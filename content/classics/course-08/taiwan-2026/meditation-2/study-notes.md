## Geshe Michael's Preface — An Introduction to Seeing Emptiness

- Geshe Michael's Preface is an introduction to his upcoming book *The Illumination of the True Thought*, being released in October, a commentary on one of Je Tsongkapa's greatest explanations of emptiness.
- The Preface is written from Geshe Michael's first-person point of view, based on his direct experience of seeing emptiness: what happened before, during, and after.
- Goal: help students understand the seeds needed to generate the experience of seeing emptiness directly.
- The book is being given away for free worldwide.

## The Four Arya Truths and the Sixteen Aspects

- **Four Arya Truths**, the four main realizations when you see emptiness directly:
- **1. Suffering**, everything that has a beginning has an end; even good things end.
- **2. Cause**, there is a reason for suffering.
- **3. End**, suffering can end.
- **4. Path**, there is a way to end suffering; there is a cause for that end.
- Each of the four truths has four parts, making sixteen aspects total.
- Very little description of these sixteen parts exists in Buddhist literature from the Buddha's time on.
- Geshe Michael provides commentary based on his own experience and teaches how to plant seeds to have all sixteen realizations. The teaching started in Malaysia and continues in these meditations.

## Investigating Your Own Story

- First step: investigate the life you received when you were born. Look at what happened and ask why it happened to you.
- Method: examine your younger years, things that happened, things you were attracted to, things you enjoyed, your family, your culture, and ask what seeds had to have been planted to create that.
- Each person investigates their own story; this investigation is the morning meditation practice.
- Different themes are used on different days. Yesterday was teachers before twelve years old; today is childhood and the senses.

## Guided Meditation — Meeting Your Future Self

- Preliminaries: 100 breaths, exhaling first.
- Invite the future version of yourself, the most perfected version you can imagine, to sit with you in meditation.
- Admire their qualities: what they have overcome, what they have let go of, and how they radiate the perfected qualities you are striving for.
- See them looking back at you, really happy and excited that you are on your way.
- Make offerings to your future self: offer your practice; confess one thing you did recently that you are not proud of; celebrate goodness you saw in somebody else.
- Ask them to give you teachings, to quickly teach you all the things you need to do to become like them; ask them to come in unexpected ways through other people.
- Ask them to stay with you, to keep inspiring, motivating, and reminding you that you are much closer than you think.
- Invite them to come into your heart and stay there always as a resource to inspire you.

## Guided Meditation — Childhood and the Senses

- Focus on childhood things that appeared and attracted you through the senses, touch, smell, sound, taste, things you were automatically attracted to for “no reason at all.”
- Timothy's childhood example: a stuffed animal with tiny feet made of slender, satin-soft fabric. Rubbing the fabric brought a deep feeling of safety and comfort.
- Using that strong childhood feeling, imagine what similar seeds could have been planted. Ideas included providing someone a soft blanket when cold, finding an abandoned cat and wrapping it up to feel safe and warm, or caring for an animal and its children.
- Practice for fifteen minutes: move through sensorily pleasurable childhood experiences; allow your mind to explore what goodness in a past life could have caused each one; then find another beautiful experience and explore it too.
- Dedicate the good seeds of the meditation to being a path for deep realizations into reality and to your future self.

## Sharing — Childhood Memories and Their Seeds

- A student remembered enjoying music programs such as MTV and Billboard every week as a child, and imagined possible seed connections to music and to Geshe Michael's love of rock and roll.
- Another student remembered a little blanket she rubbed until it wore to threads and connected the comfort to giving people hugs and nursing her baby. Her son now holds the blanket she had in college.
- Timothy remembered his mother's mac and cheese with a creamy cheese sauce. He described how, over time, this meditation can make long-forgotten memories and feelings slowly return.
